Sire & History

Prelude

Magister Lukas Brenner Clan Tremere

Viennese warlock of the broken Pyramid, washed up in Barcelona

In life you were a Jesuit-trained theologian, mossèn at the Basílica de Santa María del Mar — the Catedral del Mar of El Born, the stevedores' church the bastaixos raised stone by stone from Montjuïc. You read forbidden things to refute them: dead liturgical tongues and the apocrypha of the old Catalan scriptoria, Ripoll and Montserrat. Not an occultist. A believer. That made you exactly what a desperate warlock needed.

When Vienna fell in 2008 and the Pyramid shattered, Brenner fled west to Barcelona — far from his collapsed hierarchy — holding a palimpsest he could not read: a leaf lifted from a Ripoll codex, a lost ritual in the dead tongue you had spent your mortal life mastering. For two years he played the generous Austrian patron funding your translations, your congregation at the basilica none the wiser. Then he ran out of nights.

He took you at the altar of Santa María del Mar, beneath the great rose window, after the last Mass — not as a gift but as acquisition: a childe cannot lie to its sire and cannot leave. Before he could bind you fully he vanished — into the Carna schism or the Second Inquisition's Iberian purges, no one will say — leaving you Embraced, half-taught, and abandoned with a hunger you never asked for.

He stole the one thing a priest trades in — confession. He damned you without absolution or reckoning. That is why the guilty must confess before you punish them: every soul you force to its knees is a rehearsal for the night you find him.

What I Remember — the night at the altar
  • The cold of the altar stone of Santa María del Mar against my cheek — colder than the sea-church should have been.
  • The slim octagonal columns rising into the dark, and the great rose window gone black above me.
  • Candlelight guttering, as if a door had opened. Snuffed wax, incense, and salt off the Born underneath.
  • His voice, gentle, in German-accented Catalan, saying my name — and then the friendliness simply not being there, like a mask taken off between one word and the next.
  • Thirst. Not fear. The clearest thing of all — a drowning man's want, arriving before I knew I was dying.
  • The codex open on the lectern — Ripoll's stolen leaf — and the last thing my living eyes fixed on was my own hand in the margins.
  • The order of it — shuffled, like cards dropped and gathered wrong.
  • A second sound at the edge of the dark — a footstep on stone, a voice that wasn't his. I've never been sure it was real.
  • Whether the last thing I heard was the bells of the Born or the sea. The moment of death is simply gone — a black seam. I never said yes. I never said no.

His face was turned away at the end — so when I summon it for vengeance, I cannot quite see it. A profile, a voice, a pair of hands. To find him, I may first have to remember him. (A Taste for Blood, tasting his vitae again, may finish the memory the Embrace tore out.)

The Touchstones

p.173 · 3 mortals

Mossèn Aureli Vidal

Bound to Conviction I

Who: Your half-blind old mentor, still hearing confessions at a chapel in the Barri Gòtic where you trained.

Embodies: Mercy and forgiveness — the very thing you've perverted into a license to kill.

Bond: His remembered voice still says “this is not what I taught you.” He has no idea what you've become.

Lose him: every condemned cruelty frays the link; feeding on him shatters the Conviction outright.

Sor Pilar Esteve

Bound to Conviction II

Who: A chaplain at the Centre Penitenciari Brians who ministers to the lifers — men who will die behind those walls — hearing their confessions.

Embodies: “Confession before punishment” in its merciful key — no soul is punished unheard.

Bond: You trained alongside her; she still believes you're the man you were, and offers counsel you can't bring yourself to refuse.

Lose her: punish without a confession and you can't face her; her death removes the one mirror that lets you call your vengeance just.

Sofia Reyes · age 9

Bound to Conviction III

Who: An orphan of the Raval who shelters in Santa María del Mar, unafraid of you in a way no adult is.

Embodies: The pure, unconditional line — proof you can still protect rather than only destroy.

Bond: Your Humanity anchor. The others justify the monster; Sofia limits it.

Lose her: harm any true innocent for a Stain check — harm Sofia and Humanity goes into freefall.

A vampire keeps as many Touchstones as Convictions. Two of yours embody virtues you've corrupted (constant friction); only Sofia is a virtue you still uphold (the line whose loss actually costs you).

The Beast — The Voice

Frenzy · Compulsion
How the Beast speaks to me

My Beast does not howl. It preaches.

It wears the voice of God Almighty — my own homiletic cadence turned sovereign — and it does not beg me to feed. It commands me to commit atrocities, and calls them holy. When it rises I feel no terror and no hunger. I feel chosen. The doubt comes only after, in the ash: was that the Lord testing me, or the Devil in my blood? A believer cannot tell. A believer dare not disobey.

Fury danger frenzy

Wrath is the priest's besetting sin, and the Voice knows it. Defiance or an unrepentant sinner, and it pronounces sentence with my teeth — “Smite them. I command it.”

Perfectionism clan compulsion

The Voice demands the rite be flawless. −2 dice to all pools until a critical win or scene end (−1 on a repeat, gone on the second). It tells me the cruelty was incomplete — so I go further than I meant to.

Rötschreck terror frenzy

Fire and sun — and the Cross I flinch from. I can no longer tell holy terror from guilt; the Voice and my dead faith fight inside the same recoil.

Bane — Sundered Bonds

Since Vienna's fall my vitae can no longer Blood Bond other Kindred; others may still bind me, and binding mortals/ghouls takes extra feedings (= Bane Severity). A control-hungry warlock-priest, stripped of the one leash he most craved — the Voice's cruelest jest.

The character wants confession before punishment. The Voice wants punishment, now, of everyone. Every frenzy is God — or the Beast wearing His face — trying to skip the trial.
